# Evidence-safe cold email methods

This reference distills the supplied cold-email writing, optimization, subject-line, opening, CTA, and before/after course materials into operational rules. Apply the structures; never copy an example claim unless current evidence supports it.

## Core structure

Use this sequence:

1. subject: one credible reason to open;
2. opening: one sourced point about the recipient or their market;
3. value: one relevant outcome or capability, phrased within evidence;
4. proof: optional and only when a source or approved company fact exists;
5. CTA: one proportionate next step.

Prefer 60-140 words for a first cold email. A longer email needs a concrete reason, such as a requested technical answer. Keep paragraphs to one or two sentences.

## Subject families

Select one family that follows from the research:

- direct value or operational outcome;
- product/capability relevance;
- a real market or operational question;
- a verified pain or constraint;
- a supported differentiator;
- a timely industry topic or event;
- a specific next step such as a sample or brief call;
- sourced social proof, certification, or case evidence;
- personalization to the company, role, market, or product direction;
- educational insight;
- shared values such as sustainability;
- innovation or a new approach;
- support for a small or growing business.

Use competitor, urgency, scarcity, incentive, data-result, award, and customer-name subjects only when the exact fact is verified and approved for external use. Never invent a percentage, deadline, limited stock, market ranking, named customer, or switching claim.

Subject checklist:

- normally 3-8 words;
- clear when viewed without the body;
- no `Re:`/`Fwd:` deception;
- no all caps, excessive punctuation, or fake familiarity;
- no promise larger than the email can support.

## Opening methods

Choose one:

- specific, verifiable appreciation of the company, product, or market presence;
- a relevant question about an observed trend or operational need;
- a genuine commonality in industry, region, project type, or values;
- a sourced market insight;
- a verified analogous case or story;
- a timely, sourced trend or event;
- light tone only when appropriate to the recipient and market;
- a real trade-show or industry-event connection;
- a direct statement of the collaboration value;
- a request for feedback on one relevant issue.

Avoid `I hope this email finds you well`, generic praise, vague claims that the sender has been "following" the company, and assumptions about pain or intent.

## CTA families

Use one CTA, selected by relationship stage:

- ask a simple relevance/priority question;
- ask permission to send a catalog, case study, specification, or report;
- offer a sample or small trial when operationally approved;
- request missing business requirements;
- propose a short call only after establishing relevance;
- offer two concrete choices when both are genuinely available;
- ask which next step the recipient prefers;
- offer educational value or guidance;
- invite to a real event or visit;
- ask for a referral to the correct business contact.

Do not manufacture urgency, incentives, capacity limits, free services, dates, or rewards. A first cold email should generally ask for a reply or permission before demanding a meeting.

## Optimization pass

Compare the draft against this sequence:

1. extract the essential recipient, context, value, and action;
2. replace a generic subject with a relevant, truthful one;
3. replace a generic greeting/opening with sourced personalization;
4. move sender credentials behind recipient relevance;
5. reduce the value section to one differentiated reason to respond;
6. add proof only when available;
7. choose one CTA;
8. remove repetition, inflated adjectives, spam triggers, and long background;
9. check that every placeholder is resolved;
10. show what changed and why when optimizing an existing draft.

The course's shipment before/after example illustrates adding relevant quality-control context. Generalize the lesson: add decision-useful proof, not length for its own sake.

## Evidence ledger example

| Proposed wording | Evidence needed | Action without evidence |
|---|---|---|
| `flexible trial quantities` | approved MOQ/sample policy | ask an internal question or omit |
| `certified quality system` | current certification and allowed name | omit |
| `customers reduced returns` | approved case and exact result | use qualitative capability only |
| `production slots are limited` | current inventory/capacity record | omit |
| `your team is expanding` | recent public company source | use neutral company observation |

## Output quality

Prefer one strong primary draft. If alternatives are requested, vary the angle—not just synonyms—and label the evidence dependency of each. Never claim an email has a "high reply rate" or guaranteed conversion; these techniques are hypotheses to test, not guarantees.
