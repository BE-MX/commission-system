# Public-pool staged research framework

## 1. Enterprise knowledge grounding

Search narrowly for target industries, exclusions, product fit, buyer types, and verified sales lessons. Prefer 2–5 relevant published documents. Store only exact document/revision/version IDs; never copy internal prose into public facts or expose it to a search provider.

## 2. Industry and identity gate

Use low-cost business signals first: Ark's frozen seed, opened official pages, public business/social profiles, visible services/products, registries, booking/store pages, and historical summaries already exposed by Ark.

- `core`: directly sells, installs, teaches, distributes, brands, or repeatedly discusses the target category.
- `adjacent`: serves a compatible buyer/use case, but the target category is not proven core.
- `uncertain`: entity or business scope remains sparse or ambiguous.
- `irrelevant`: reliable opened evidence establishes an unrelated business with no plausible target use.

Missing websites, free mailboxes, inactive accounts, generic names, photos, country, race, or failed searches never prove irrelevance. `irrelevant` stops the task before deeper collection.

## 3. Identity and public-social research

When a website is absent or weak, public business profiles, booking/store pages, Google Business, registries, and commercial directories may provide operating evidence. A matching name or handle is only a candidate. Prefer two compatible anchors such as a reciprocal link, business name, public role, city, logo, public business contact, or consistent service content.

A personal mailbox or contact name is a clue, not a company join key. Never search private relationships, combine a private address/email with public queries, or merge two same-name people. If Ark exposes a coarse approved location hint, combine it only with a business name in at most 2–3 searches; it is not a fact and cannot establish identity alone.

Tier focus:

- T1: current operating state, evidence-backed change since Ark history, reactivation questions, and relationship risk.
- T2: product fit, buyer type, public purchasing role, supplier/switch evidence, and active channels.
- T3: light identity/business verification; deepen only after a credible business anchor.
- High-score acquisition: independently verify source identity, target fit, buying signals, reachability, and risks; the discovery score is routing input, not proof.

## 4. Result boundaries

Capture atomic source facts before any inference. Inferred facts must cite supporting fact IDs and a rule version. Build claims only for the supported `customer_research_v1` sections: `identity`, `business_quality`, `product_fit`, `supplier_status`, `risk`, and `strategy`.

Use `unknown` when public evidence cannot establish supplier stability, purchase volume, budget, decision authority, payment compliance, or intent. Research may suggest low-risk questions for sales, but it must not assign a qualification grade, probability, or customer admission decision. Human review of research quality and later customer qualification are distinct Ark steps.
